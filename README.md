# Comprehensive Bioinformatics Analysis of the Human PTEN Gene and Protein

An independent **in silico bioinformatics project** investigating the human **PTEN (Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog)** gene and protein using publicly available biological databases and computational tools.

---

## 🧬 Overview

PTEN (Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog) is one of the most extensively studied tumour suppressor genes in humans. It plays a central role in regulating the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway, thereby controlling cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, metabolism, and genomic stability.

This project presents a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis of the human PTEN gene and protein by integrating genomic, structural, functional, interaction, pathway, disease association, therapeutic target, and cancer expression analyses using publicly available databases and computational tools.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Retrieve and annotate the PTEN gene.
- Analyse genomic organization and transcript information.
- Retrieve the canonical PTEN protein sequence.
- Determine physicochemical properties.
- Identify conserved protein domains.
- Analyse secondary and tertiary structures.
- Construct protein–protein interaction networks.
- Perform Gene Ontology enrichment analysis.
- Analyse KEGG and Reactome pathways.
- Investigate disease-associated variants.
- Explore PTEN as a therapeutic target.
- Evaluate PTEN expression and survival across multiple cancers.

## 📊 Major Findings

- PTEN is located on chromosome **10q23.31**.
- The canonical PTEN protein contains **403 amino acids**.
- Conserved Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase (PTP) and C2 domains were identified.
- PTEN interacts with multiple proteins involved in PI3K/AKT signalling.
- Functional enrichment highlighted roles in phosphoinositide metabolism, apoptosis, cell cycle regulation, and tumour suppression.
- ClinVar and OMIM analyses demonstrated strong associations with hereditary syndromes and multiple cancers.
- Therapeutic Target Database identified PTEN as an important therapeutic target.
- GEPIA3 analysis demonstrated the clinical significance of PTEN expression and overall survival across human cancers.

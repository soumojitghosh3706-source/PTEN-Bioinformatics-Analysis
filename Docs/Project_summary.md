# Comprehensive Bioinformatics Analysis of the Human PTEN Gene and Protein

## Project Summary

### Abstract

The **Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog (PTEN)** gene is one of the most extensively studied tumour suppressor genes because of its critical role in regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, metabolism, and genomic stability. Loss or inactivation of PTEN contributes to the development of numerous cancers through dysregulation of the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway. This project presents a comprehensive **in silico bioinformatics analysis** of the human PTEN gene and its encoded protein using publicly available biological databases and computational tools. The study integrates gene annotation, sequence analysis, protein characterization, structural prediction, protein–protein interaction analysis, functional enrichment, disease association, therapeutic target exploration, and pan-cancer expression profiling to provide a systematic understanding of PTEN structure, function, and clinical significance.

---

# Introduction

PTEN is located on chromosome **10q23.31** and encodes a dual-specificity phosphatase that primarily converts phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP₃) into phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate (PIP₂). Through this activity, PTEN negatively regulates the PI3K/AKT signalling pathway and plays a fundamental role in controlling cell growth, survival, migration, metabolism, and apoptosis. Alterations in PTEN are associated with several inherited disorders and numerous malignancies, including glioblastoma, breast cancer, prostate cancer, melanoma, and endometrial carcinoma. Owing to its biological and clinical importance, PTEN serves as an excellent model for comprehensive bioinformatics analysis.

# Objectives

The objectives of this study were to:

- Retrieve and annotate the human PTEN gene.
- Identify the canonical transcript and coding sequence.
- Perform Open Reading Frame (ORF) analysis.
- Characterize the physicochemical properties of the PTEN protein.
- Identify conserved functional domains.
- Predict secondary and tertiary protein structures.
- Investigate protein–protein interactions.
- Perform Gene Ontology and pathway enrichment analyses.
- Explore disease-associated variants and therapeutic relevance.
- Evaluate PTEN gene expression and survival associations across human cancers.

---

# Methodology

The study employed a systematic bioinformatics workflow integrating fifteen publicly available databases and computational tools.

Gene annotation and genomic organization were obtained using **NCBI Gene** and the **Genome Data Viewer (GDV)**. The canonical transcript and protein sequences were retrieved from **RefSeq** and **UniProt**, followed by coding sequence verification using **NCBI ORF Finder**. Physicochemical properties were analysed using **ExPASy ProtParam**, while conserved domains were identified using the **NCBI Conserved Domain Database (CDD)**.

Protein structural analyses were performed using the **AlphaFold Protein Structure Database** together with **DSSP** for secondary structure assignment. Functional protein interactions were investigated using the **STRING** database. Biological process, molecular function, cellular component, KEGG pathway, and Reactome pathway enrichment analyses were carried out using **ShinyGO** and **Reactome**.

Disease associations and clinically significant variants were explored using **OMIM** and **ClinVar**, whereas therapeutic relevance was examined using the **Therapeutic Target Database (TTD)**. Finally, **GEPIA3** was used to analyse PTEN expression patterns and overall survival across multiple cancer types using TCGA and GTEx datasets.

# Key Findings

The analysis confirmed that the canonical PTEN transcript (**NM_000314.8**) encodes a **403-amino-acid** protein (**NP_000305.3**) containing a catalytic phosphatase (PTP) domain and a C2 membrane-binding domain.

Physicochemical analysis demonstrated that PTEN is an acidic and hydrophilic protein with a molecular weight of approximately **47.17 kDa** and a theoretical isoelectric point of **5.94**.

AlphaFold predicted a highly reliable three-dimensional structure consisting of a well-defined phosphatase domain, a β-sheet-rich C2 domain, and a flexible C-terminal regulatory region. DSSP analysis further demonstrated that the protein adopts a mixed α/β architecture.

Protein–protein interaction analysis identified PTEN as a central regulatory hub interacting with proteins involved in PI3K/AKT signalling, apoptosis, cell adhesion, and tumour suppression.

Gene Ontology and pathway enrichment analyses revealed significant enrichment of biological processes associated with phosphatidylinositol signalling, protein dephosphorylation, cell proliferation, apoptosis, immune regulation, and multiple cancer-related pathways.

Disease association analyses using OMIM and ClinVar demonstrated strong associations between PTEN mutations and PTEN Hamartoma Tumour Syndrome (PHTS), Cowden syndrome, glioblastoma, prostate cancer, melanoma, endometrial carcinoma, and several additional hereditary and sporadic malignancies.

Gene expression and survival analyses indicated that PTEN expression varies across different cancer types and may possess prognostic significance depending on tumour context.

# Conclusion

This project demonstrates the effectiveness of integrating multiple publicly available bioinformatics resources to perform a comprehensive molecular characterization of a clinically important human gene. The analyses collectively describe the genomic organization, protein structure, physicochemical characteristics, interaction networks, functional pathways, disease associations, therapeutic relevance, and expression profile of PTEN. The study highlights the importance of computational biology in supporting molecular research and provides a valuable reference framework for future experimental investigations involving PTEN.
